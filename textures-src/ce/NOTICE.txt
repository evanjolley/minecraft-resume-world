`block/` is a subset of that pack's assets/minecraft/textures/block, copied
unmodified. It holds exactly the textures scripts/build-textures.mjs needs:
the ones src/blocks.js names, plus the substitute bases the build derives
1.17+ blocks from. Adding a block that wants a texture which isn't here makes
the build fail by name rather than render as checkerboard.

`item/` is the same arrangement for that pack's assets/minecraft/textures/item:
an 89-file subset of the sprites src/items.js names. It grew by five when
potions landed -- potion.png, potion_overlay.png, splash_potion.png,
glass_bottle.png and milk_bucket.png -- all copied unmodified from the same
pack and the same commit as everything else here.

The potion sprites are the only two-layer art in the set. The build composites
them: potion_overlay.png is greyscale and is multiplied by the potion's colour,
and potion.png (or splash_potion.png) goes over the top untinted. That is
exactly what vanilla's models/item/potion.json describes and what Minecraft
does at render time, so the result is CC-BY-SA-4.0 like every other derivative
below.

Modification: crack.png is a derivative of the pack's crack_anylength.png.
Its RGB channels have been flattened to black while preserving the original
alpha, so it can be used as a pure alpha mask for a black crack overlay.

Modification: the build derives further textures at runtime, none of which are
committed. Pixel Perfection CE predates Caves & Cliffs, so it has no copper,
deepslate, tuff, calcite, sculk, froglights, mangrove/cherry/pale oak/bamboo
wood or resin. Those blocks are drawn as colour-multiplied derivatives of the
nearest CE texture -- see CE_SUBSTITUTES in scripts/build-textures.mjs for the
full list of what is derived from what. Grass and most leaves are likewise
multiplied by a biome colour, which is what Minecraft itself does at render
time.

All of those derivatives are likewise CC-BY-SA-4.0.
